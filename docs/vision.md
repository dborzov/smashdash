# Vision & intent

This document records **what the owner asked for and why**, so future work (by people or AI
agents) stays true to it. When in doubt, re-read this before changing direction.

## The one-line pitch

Smashdash is a story-driven fantasy RPG that teaches bright kids (roughly grades 3–7) the
*point* of algebra — and the core skills behind it — by making them the hero who un-muddles a
kingdom where every number has been stuffed into a box.

## Who it is for

- Smart, curious kids aged ~8–12 who already know basic arithmetic and fractions.
- They play mostly on **iPads** (touch) and **desktop** browsers.
- English only.

## What the owner wants (from the original brief)

1. **A static website** on GitHub Pages. No backend, no LLM at runtime. Everything is
   scripted: content is authored in advance (by AI and humans) and practice is generated by
   random number generators.
2. **Player state in the browser** (localStorage): progress, hero, XP, gold, loot, pet.
   **Multiple player profiles** in one browser, switchable.
3. **Gamification, Duolingo-style in function, RPG / D&D / choose-your-own-adventure in
   flavour**: create a character, travel to locations (each loosely matching a topic), win
   adventures by solving things, random encounters to practise old material, XP, gold, loot,
   a daily XP goal and streaks, and a tamagotchi-style pet to spend gold on.
4. **Visually stunning and atmospheric**: location-specific art, interactive visualisations,
   HTML5/JS for everything, levelling up and unlocking.
5. **Story-motivated explanations** before new topics, with interactive story parts that
   **constantly check the player is following** (small checks every couple of lines).
6. **A well-structured learning plan** built from **small atomic skills**, explicitly targeting
   the typical stumbling blocks. Example from the owner: practise *reading expressions*
   ("x = 4, what is 3x + 7?") on its own; practise *doing the same operation to both sides* on
   its own, before combining it with deciding which operation to use.
7. **Good RNG practice generators** with varied mini-game patterns, tied to the story.
8. **Beautiful maths rendering** (proper LaTeX, clearly visible, good colours).
9. **Scientific notation and powers of ten**, including negative powers like $10^{-3}$.
10. **Depth over breadth**: "be selective… choose some specific interconnected concepts and
    skills that tend to be hard for kids, and do that specific job well."

## Teach, don't quiz (owner feedback, October 2026)

The owner's verdict on the first version of story problems: the app presented multi-step
problems "as an idiotic multiple choice question for the final answer", the hint was a
"helpful" *use x*, and the app "never ever even visualizes, shows as an example and lets the
kid give a try at the key learning material": **the intermediate steps** — writing the
equation, finding out whether it is right or wrong, then doing transformations to both sides
to solve it. "Is the kid supposed to do everything in his head? How is the app even helping
and guiding and teaching the kid… about both how to approach the problem, and the *show your
work* skills?"

What the owner wants instead, and what every future change should keep:

- **"Interactive and visually rich functionality for every little step"**, guiding the kid,
  with **feedback on every step**; when something is wrong, **show where they went wrong and
  how to make it work**.
- **"The point is to teach, to explain, to provide intuition and intuitive understanding…
  so much more than just a multiple choice maths quiz."**
- **Show it as an example, then let the kid try** — the same tool for both.
- The tool is the **EQcanvas**: drag the operators, $x$ and the story's numbers onto a canvas
  with instant feedback and a text reflection of what was built; once the equation is right,
  apply tools to it (subtract a number or $x$ from both sides, divide both sides, …) and
  tweak them. It should be "adjusted and adapted for each side" (each place it is used) and
  **"adopted and implemented across this tutorial website"** — not a one-off for story
  problems.

So: never ask only for the final answer of a multi-step problem; never give a generic hint;
make every step visible, checkable and undoable. How this is built: `learning-design.md`
(*EQcanvas*), `decisions.md` (26–27).

## Tone — this matters a lot

- **Playful, humorous, a bit risqué.** Poop jokes, farts, burps, a sarcastic frog mentor,
  black humour (a pet can die, with flies buzzing over it). Kid-appropriate, never mean.
- **Alive, creative, talented — never dry.** "The formality and dryness of formal education is
  poison we want to avoid." No curriculum codes, no worksheets, no grey textbook voice.
- **Forgiving but consequential.** A wrong-but-close answer gets a nudge ("close — check the
  sign!") and a retry before it counts as wrong. But things *matter*: hearts run out, streaks
  break, pets can die, shields are expensive and limited.

## Explicit non-goals (for now)

- No parent dashboard / teacher features in v1 (planned later, see `roadmap.md`).
- No accounts, servers, analytics or LLM calls.
- Not trying to cover a whole curriculum.
